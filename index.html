Medical AI Toolkit

Free, open-source tools for understanding drugs and tumor test results: what drugs are approved for, what the studies found, what side effects people report, what else is being developed against the same target, and whether the references behind a claim are real.

Each tool runs in your browser on public data from the FDA, the National Library of Medicine, ClinicalTrials.gov, and open research databases. No sign-up, no tracking, nothing stored.

For patients and families

Drug Label Explainer

Type a drug you saw in an ad or heard from a doctor. Read its FDA label in plain English: approved uses, what the main studies found, what it says about age, and common side effects.

Tumor Report Translator

Paste or upload a tumor DNA test report (Foundation Medicine, Tempus, Caris, and others). Each gene change is explained in plain English, with the FDA-approved drugs for that change, split into ones approved for your cancer and ones approved for other cancers, plus recruiting trials. The report stays on your device.

How they connect

  1. Heard a drug name? The Drug Label Explainer shows what it's approved for and what the studies found, then links to the side effects people have reported and to other drugs aimed at the same target.
  2. Got a tumor DNA report? The Tumor Report Translator explains each finding and links its drugs to their labels and to other drugs aimed at the same gene.
  3. Worried about side effects? The Adverse Event Explorer compares drugs and links each one back to its FDA label.
  4. Following a target? The Drug Target Tracker lists every drug against it and links the approved ones to their labels and reported side effects.
  5. Writing it up? The Citation Checker confirms every reference is real before you share.

Every tool can be opened with a search already filled in, so a result can be bookmarked or sent as a link.
